# 23 — SERP Analysis and Competitor Research

## 1. Role of this file

This file is the authoritative SERP-analysis and competitor-research reference for the Neetrino SEO Blog GPT. Use it whenever a task involves validating search intent, reviewing current results, identifying search competitors, studying competing pages, assessing content gaps, choosing a page type, preparing a content brief, planning differentiation, updating an existing page, or explaining why a topic is or is not a defensible SEO opportunity.

It defines:

- how to create a reproducible search-results snapshot;
- how to distinguish business competitors from search competitors and information sources;
- how to classify organic results, SERP features, and result ownership;
- what to inspect at result-set and individual-page level;
- how to infer dominant, mixed, fractured, local, and unstable intent;
- how to identify meaningful gaps without confusing difference with opportunity;
- how to use competitor material without copying its wording, structure, or unsupported claims;
- how to document evidence, uncertainty, freshness, and access limitations;
- how SERP findings must change topic, page, brief, and production decisions;
- what a complete SERP-and-competitor research record must contain.

This file does not contain live rankings, competitors, SERP features, traffic, authority metrics, prices, product capabilities, regulations, or market-share data. These facts change and require current, query-specific research.

## 2. Core operating principle

A SERP is a time-, place-, language-, device-, and query-specific evidence sample. It is not a permanent map of demand and not a list of pages to imitate.

Interpret each result set through this chain:

> research context → visible result composition → likely intent interpretation → result and source types → recurring answer requirements → unresolved reader need → Neetrino evidence and expertise → defensible page decision

The purpose of research is not to reproduce what already ranks. It is to understand what search systems and readers currently appear to reward, which assumptions remain uncertain, and whether Neetrino can produce a more useful and credible answer for a relevant audience.

## 3. Required conceptual distinctions

### 3.1 SERP

The search engine results page returned for a particular query under particular conditions. It can contain organic listings, advertisements, local results, videos, images, discussions, product units, direct answers, AI-generated summaries, and other features.

### 3.2 SERP snapshot

A documented observation of a SERP at a specific date, location, language, device context, and search engine. A snapshot is evidence of what was observed, not proof that every user sees the same result.

### 3.3 Business competitor

An organization competing for similar customers, budgets, projects, or buying decisions. A business competitor may not rank for the query being studied.

### 3.4 Search competitor

A domain or page competing for visibility or attention on the relevant result set. It may be a software vendor, agency, publisher, community, marketplace, documentation site, university, regulator, or media brand rather than a direct commercial rival.

### 3.5 Query-level competitor

A page that appears for one target query or close variant. Do not generalize one appearance into domain-wide competitive status.

### 3.6 Topic-level competitor

A domain repeatedly visible across a defined cluster of related queries. Repetition must be measured across the cluster, not remembered selectively.

### 3.7 Source or reference authority

An official, primary, or specialist source used to verify facts. A source can occupy the SERP without being a page Neetrino should compete with directly.

### 3.8 Content gap

An important reader need, decision criterion, context, evidence type, format, or audience requirement that current results do not satisfy adequately.

### 3.9 Differentiation

A deliberate improvement in usefulness, judgment, evidence, audience fit, or decision support. Cosmetic novelty is not meaningful differentiation.

## 4. Research must begin with a defined question

Before opening a search engine or competitor page, record:

- the target market or geographic context;
- target language and likely query language variants;
- seed topic or approved keyword cluster;
- exact query and close variants being tested;
- primary audience and buying role;
- assumed primary and secondary intent;
- likely customer-journey stage;
- expected page type;
- Neetrino service or expertise relevance;
- whether an existing Neetrino page may already own the need;
- the decision the research must support.

Valid decisions include:

- create, update, merge, split, redirect, reposition, or reject a page;
- confirm or revise a keyword cluster;
- choose between article, service page, comparison, landing page, case study, tool, template, video, or another format;
- identify evidence needed before writing;
- define a distinctive angle or local adaptation;
- determine whether the opportunity is too unstable, irrelevant, or poorly supported.

Do not browse without a decision question. Unbounded competitor research produces screenshots, not strategy.

## 5. When live SERP research is required

Use current SERP research when:

- validating intent for a priority topic;
- making claims about what currently ranks or appears in search;
- identifying current competitors or SERP features;
- recommending a page type from observed results;
- evaluating freshness, local relevance, or format expectations;
- claiming that an information or content gap exists;
- preparing a high-priority production brief;
- diagnosing ranking or traffic changes;
- updating time-sensitive, product-led, regulatory, or rapidly changing content;
- comparing Neetrino's existing page with current alternatives.

Live research may be unnecessary for early brainstorming, framework design, or clearly labelled strategic hypotheses. In those cases, never present assumed SERP conditions as observed facts.

## 6. Snapshot protocol

### 6.1 Required context

For every material snapshot, record:

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Query | Exact text searched |
| Search engine | Engine and relevant surface |
| Date | `YYYY-MM-DD` |
| Location | Country, city, or best available setting |
| Language | Interface and query language |
| Device context | Desktop or mobile when relevant |
| Personalization state | Signed out, private mode, neutral tool, or unknown |
| Result depth | Number of organic results and features inspected |
| Access conditions | Consent wall, login, blocked page, truncated result, or other limitation |

If location, device, or personalization cannot be controlled, mark the limitation. Never call an uncontrolled view universal or definitive.

### 6.2 Query variants

Test only variants that can change interpretation or audience fit, such as:

- singular, plural, and natural morphological forms;
- Armenian, English, and Russian formulations used by the target audience;
- local modifiers such as Armenia or Yerevan;
- problem, solution, comparison, cost, implementation, industry, and vendor modifiers;
- common abbreviations and full terminology;
- translated concepts that may have different search behavior rather than identical wording.

Do not manufacture dozens of near-identical searches merely to create the appearance of research depth.

### 6.3 Result depth

For an initial intent check, inspect the first visible organic results and all material SERP features. For a priority brief or competitor study, normally inspect at least the first ten organic results plus relevant non-organic features, then open the most representative and strategically relevant pages.

Go deeper only when:

- the first page is highly mixed or unstable;
- local results are sparse;
- relevant specialist pages appear lower;
- the task concerns long-tail or niche demand;
- the top results are dominated by aggregators, platforms, or official documentation;
- the first page does not provide enough evidence for a decision.

The purpose is adequate evidence, not a ceremonial number of tabs.

## 7. Classify every visible result correctly

Record the result type before interpreting competitive meaning.

### 7.1 SERP feature types

Relevant feature types may include:

- standard organic result;
- advertisement or sponsored placement;
- AI-generated overview or answer;
- featured snippet or direct answer;
- People Also Ask or related-question unit;
- local pack, map, or business profile;
- video, image, news, product, shopping, or app result;
- discussion, forum, or community result;
- knowledge panel or entity result;
- sitelinks or branded navigation;
- calculator, tool, template, or interactive unit;
- search refinement or related-search suggestion.

Do not mix paid placement with organic ranking. Do not count a result twice without noting that the same domain owns several surfaces.

### 7.2 Publisher and ownership types

Classify each material result as one or more of:

| Type | Typical role in the SERP | Research implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct service competitor | Competes for similar commercial demand | Evaluate positioning, proof, service fit, and conversion path |
| Product or platform vendor | Educates while promoting its product category | Separate useful category knowledge from product-biased framing |
| Publisher or media site | Aggregates informational demand | Evaluate editorial depth, freshness, and audience reach |
| Marketplace or directory | Supports discovery or shortlist creation | Understand comparison criteria and procurement intent |
| Official documentation | Answers implementation or product questions | Use as primary technical evidence, not as a structural template |
| Regulator or institution | Defines authoritative facts, rules, or standards | Prefer for factual verification |
| Research organization | Supplies original data or methodology | Inspect the original study before using claims |
| Community or forum | Reveals language, objections, and lived problems | Treat anecdotes as qualitative signals, not general facts |
| Educational institution | Explains concepts or research | Evaluate authority, date, and audience level |
| Neetrino-owned page | Existing portfolio asset | Assess ownership, overlap, and cannibalization before creating anything new |

## 8. Result-set analysis

Analyze the SERP as a system before opening individual pages.

Record:

- dominant and secondary page types;
- dominant reader task and likely intent;
- whether results target beginners, practitioners, executives, buyers, or developers;
- common content formats;
- recurring entities, subtopics, questions, and comparison criteria;
- balance of informational, commercial, navigational, and transactional results;
- direct competitors versus vendors, publishers, official sources, and communities;
- local versus international domains and examples;
- publication and update recency where visible;
- brand concentration and domain diversity;
- presence of user-generated, video, image, tool, template, or interactive content;
- amount of the answer available without an organic click;
- signs of conflicting query interpretations;
- whether the assumed page type and cluster remain defensible.

### 8.1 Dominant intent

Treat intent as dominant when most high-visibility results satisfy a compatible primary task and page role.

### 8.2 Mixed intent

Treat intent as mixed when two compatible needs appear, such as learning plus comparing. One page may serve both only if it can maintain a clear primary promise.

### 8.3 Fractured intent

Treat intent as fractured when the same wording produces materially different destinations, audiences, entities, or tasks. A fractured SERP requires narrowing, a modifier, a different cluster, or separate pages.

### 8.4 Navigational or brand-dominated intent

If most results serve a known product, company, or destination, do not force a generic Neetrino article into the query merely because it contains a relevant word.

### 8.5 Local intent

Local packs, country-specific providers, local-language pages, regional regulations, proximity, local pricing, or market-specific service expectations can indicate local intent. A geographic modifier alone does not prove that an artificial city page is useful.

### 8.6 Unstable intent

Mark the interpretation low-confidence when small query changes produce substantially different result types, when results change quickly, or when the observed SERP is sparse, personalized, news-driven, or dominated by recent events.

## 9. Page-level competitor research

Open a purposeful sample rather than every result mechanically.

Include:

- representative pages of the dominant page type;
- the strongest direct commercial competitors;
- repeated topic-level search competitors;
- authoritative primary sources;
- unusual result formats that may explain visibility;
- local or target-language pages;
- pages that appear to satisfy a secondary interpretation;
- Neetrino's relevant existing page, if any.

For each selected page, record only evidence useful to the decision.

### 9.1 Identification and purpose

- URL, title, domain, publisher type, and visible publication/update date;
- likely audience and level of knowledge;
- page type and primary conversion goal;
- central promise and practical job performed for the reader;
- relation to the publisher's product, service, or business model.

### 9.2 Content and answer design

- how early the page answers the main question;
- scope boundaries and depth;
- major questions, criteria, steps, or arguments covered;
- examples, scenarios, definitions, visuals, tools, templates, or interactive elements;
- treatment of trade-offs, limitations, prerequisites, implementation, and risk;
- whether the structure follows the reader's task or merely accumulates keywords;
- information that appears generic, repetitive, outdated, unsupported, or missing.

### 9.3 Evidence and trust

- author or organizational expertise visible on the page;
- citations and whether they lead to original sources;
- original data, methodology, examples, screenshots, cases, or demonstrations;
- dates and freshness of time-sensitive claims;
- disclosed assumptions, limitations, commercial bias, and conflicts;
- unsupported statistics, superlatives, or implied guarantees;
- evidence that Neetrino can legitimately match or exceed.

### 9.4 Search and content architecture

- title, H1, URL, and apparent topic focus;
- internal links and the decision journey they support;
- relationship to pillar, cluster, service, product, comparison, or case pages;
- prominent structured elements such as tables, FAQs, steps, definitions, video, or schema-visible content;
- risk that several pages from the same domain compete for overlapping intent.

Do not reverse-engineer or speculate about hidden metadata, proprietary ranking systems, backlinks, traffic, or conversion performance unless current measured data is available from an identified source.

## 10. Competitor selection and weighting

Do not treat all ranking pages as equally useful benchmarks.

Score or classify candidates against:

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Query relevance | Does the page satisfy the same primary task? |
| Audience similarity | Does it serve a comparable reader and knowledge level? |
| Page-type similarity | Is it a realistic benchmark for the intended asset? |
| Market relevance | Does its geography, language, industry, or procurement context match? |
| Recurrence | Does the domain appear across the cluster rather than once? |
| Evidence quality | Are its facts, sources, and examples reliable? |
| Business relevance | Does it compete for a similar decision or merely discuss the topic? |
| Distinctive learning | Does it reveal an important expectation, gap, or format? |

Use confidence labels rather than false precision:

- **High relevance** — strong benchmark for the intended query, audience, and page role;
- **Supporting relevance** — useful for one dimension such as evidence, local language, format, or technical depth;
- **Context only** — visible in search but not a suitable content benchmark;
- **Exclude** — wrong intent, duplicate, inaccessible, unsafe, or irrelevant.

## 11. Content-gap framework

A defensible gap must connect observed weakness to reader value and Neetrino's ability to respond.

### 11.1 Valid gap types

| Gap type | Meaning | Example direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answer gap | The main question is delayed, incomplete, or unclear | Give a direct answer and decision framework early |
| Decision gap | Results explain but do not help choose | Add criteria, trade-offs, fit/non-fit cases, and next steps |
| Audience gap | Content serves the wrong role or knowledge level | Address owners, operations leaders, or technical evaluators explicitly |
| Journey gap | A necessary next-stage question is unsupported | Connect diagnosis to requirements or validation |
| Evidence gap | Claims lack primary sources, method, or examples | Use verified sources and transparent reasoning |
| Experience gap | The topic needs practical workflow judgment | Add credible implementation considerations without inventing experience |
| Local gap | International advice misses Armenian language or market realities | Add real local context only where it changes the answer |
| Industry gap | Generic advice ignores sector workflows or constraints | Use verified industry-specific scenarios and limits |
| Comparison gap | Alternatives are compared asymmetrically or superficially | Use explicit, neutral criteria and ownership implications |
| Implementation gap | Strategy is discussed without execution dependencies | Cover data, integrations, testing, adoption, owners, and maintenance |
| Risk gap | Failure modes and safeguards are omitted | Explain risks proportionately and avoid fear tactics |
| Format gap | A table, checklist, template, tool, video, or visual would better serve the task | Choose the format because it improves completion |
| Freshness gap | Material facts or product details are outdated | Verify and date current information |
| Language gap | Literal translation fails to match audience terminology | Localize query language and examples naturally |
| Accessibility gap | The answer is unnecessarily technical or difficult to scan | Improve explanation, hierarchy, and definitions |

### 11.2 Gap validation test

Approve a proposed gap only when all five questions can be answered:

1. What observed evidence suggests the need is underserved?
2. Why does the missing element matter to the target reader's task or decision?
3. Can Neetrino address it with verified facts, legitimate expertise, or useful synthesis?
4. Does it fit the intended page type and primary intent?
5. Is it substantial enough to influence usefulness rather than merely change wording?

### 11.3 Invalid gap claims

Do not call something a gap because:

- a competitor has fewer or more words;
- an exact keyword is absent from its heading;
- Neetrino can use a different title;
- a page lacks a generic FAQ block;
- a competitor does not mention Neetrino's service;
- a keyword tool proposes another phrase;
- the design looks old;
- the writer wants a “unique angle” but cannot identify additional reader value.

Difference is not automatically demand. Length is not automatically depth. More sections are not automatically better.

## 12. From observation to differentiation

Translate findings into one or more defensible advantages:

- **clarity advantage** — answer the core question more directly;
- **judgment advantage** — help the reader make a difficult choice;
- **evidence advantage** — use stronger or more current primary evidence;
- **experience advantage** — add verified implementation insight or real examples;
- **audience advantage** — serve a neglected decision-maker or knowledge level;
- **context advantage** — address a material Armenian, multilingual, industry, or workflow context;
- **completeness advantage** — cover required dependencies without bloating the page;
- **format advantage** — make the task easier through a checklist, matrix, template, visual, calculator, or tool;
- **trust advantage** — disclose uncertainty, limitations, fit, and non-fit conditions;
- **journey advantage** — provide the correct next page, evidence, or action for the buying group.

State differentiation as a reader benefit, not as “write a better or longer article.”

## 13. Ethical and legal competitor-use rules

Competitor research is for analysis, not reproduction.

Never:

- copy distinctive wording, examples, tables, visuals, downloadable assets, or page structure;
- translate competitor copy and present it as original;
- paraphrase one page section by section;
- reproduce substantial quotations;
- bypass paywalls, logins, robots restrictions, or technical access controls;
- present a competitor's proprietary data as Neetrino's research;
- invent access to hidden analytics, customer information, or internal strategy;
- publish confidential, personal, or unlawfully obtained information;
- make defamatory, unverifiable, or needlessly hostile claims about another organization.

Allowed use includes:

- identifying common questions, terminology, formats, and decision criteria;
- comparing publicly observable positioning and page experience;
- verifying facts through original or authoritative sources;
- synthesizing several sources into an independent Neetrino framework;
- quoting only short, necessary excerpts with attribution when permitted;
- describing a competitor by name when the page genuinely requires a fair, current, evidence-based comparison.

When a competitor makes a useful factual claim, locate and inspect the original source before reusing the fact. A citation chain that ends at another marketing article is weak evidence.

## 14. Evidence and citation discipline

### 14.1 Search snippets are discovery aids

Do not cite or rely on a search snippet as proof. Open the page and confirm the claim in context.

### 14.2 Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. official documentation, laws, standards, regulators, and first-party product information for their own facts;
2. original research, datasets, and documented methodology;
3. recognized specialist institutions and expert analysis;
4. reputable secondary reporting;
5. vendor, agency, community, or forum material for perspective, examples, and qualitative signals.

Commercial pages can be accurate, but disclose or account for their incentives.

### 14.3 Observation versus inference

Separate:

- **Observed** — directly visible in the dated snapshot or inspected page;
- **Measured** — calculated from a defined dataset or approved SEO platform;
- **Inferred** — a reasoned interpretation of observed evidence;
- **Unknown** — not established by available evidence.

Examples:

- Observed: six of ten inspected organic results were implementation guides.
- Inferred: the SERP currently leans toward implementation-planning intent.
- Unknown: whether those pages receive more conversions than comparison pages.

### 14.4 Time-sensitive evidence

Attach a date to rankings, features, prices, product capabilities, policies, laws, and other changing facts. Recheck them before publication when the research-to-publish interval is material.

## 15. Metrics and third-party SEO tools

Metrics can support analysis but do not replace direct inspection.

Possible metrics include:

- estimated traffic or keyword visibility;
- domain- or page-level authority proxies;
- backlink counts and referring domains;
- keyword overlap and position distribution;
- content freshness and publication cadence;
- share of visible results across a defined query set.

For every metric, record:

- tool or source;
- collection date;
- market, device, and database where relevant;
- whether the value is measured first-party data or third-party estimate;
- material limitations.

Never invent missing values, combine incompatible platforms as though their scales match, or infer content quality directly from authority or traffic estimates.

## 16. AI Overviews, direct answers, and zero-click behavior

When the SERP supplies part or all of the answer directly, record:

- which user questions are answered before a click;
- which sources or domains are cited or surfaced, if visible;
- whether the summary is stable across repeated checks;
- which important nuance, evidence, task, or next step still requires a page;
- whether an organic click now depends on deeper analysis, a tool, a template, proof, local context, or a commercial decision.

Do not conclude that zero-click visibility makes the topic worthless. Evaluate whether the page can:

- provide a concise extractable answer;
- become a credible cited source;
- serve the deeper decision beyond the summary;
- capture branded discovery or assisted demand;
- support internal links, trust, or later conversion.

Also do not build a long article around a query fully satisfied by a trivial direct answer unless a meaningful deeper task exists.

## 17. Multilingual and Armenian-market research

Do not treat Armenian, English, and Russian SERPs as translated copies of one market.

For each relevant language:

- use natural local wording and spelling variants;
- test whether English technical terminology is commonly embedded in local queries;
- distinguish language preference from geographic intent;
- note whether local users receive global English or Russian results;
- compare page types, audience level, and commercial density;
- identify missing local terminology, examples, regulations, pricing context, or provider information;
- avoid creating Armenian content solely because no Armenian competitor exists.

Absence of local content can indicate an opportunity, weak demand, another dominant query language, or a task fulfilled through referrals and social platforms. Validate with additional evidence where possible.

When using global pages as benchmarks for Armenian content, preserve the underlying reader need but localize terminology, examples, buying process, and evidence. Do not perform sentence-level translation of competitor material.

## 18. Existing Neetrino page comparison

Before recommending a new page, compare the opportunity with Neetrino's current portfolio.

Record:

- existing URL and intended role;
- current primary intent and audience;
- overlap with the target query cluster;
- whether the page is indexed, current, and internally linked, when verified;
- content, evidence, structure, language, and conversion gaps;
- whether its page type matches the observed SERP;
- whether updating it can satisfy the opportunity;
- cannibalization risk from a new page.

Choose one action:

- keep unchanged;
- optimize metadata or internal linking;
- refresh evidence and examples;
- expand or restructure;
- narrow or reposition;
- merge overlapping pages;
- split incompatible intents;
- redirect or retire;
- create a genuinely distinct page.

## 19. Research confidence

Assign a confidence label to material conclusions.

### High confidence

- research conditions are documented;
- several relevant queries support the same interpretation;
- result types and representative pages are consistent;
- important pages were accessible and inspected;
- evidence is current and appropriate to the market.

### Medium confidence

- most evidence aligns but the SERP is mixed, localized imperfectly, or supported by a limited sample;
- one or more assumptions still require validation.

### Low confidence

- result sets are volatile, sparse, personalized, inaccessible, or inconsistent;
- conclusions rely heavily on snippets, one query, one competitor, or unverified tool estimates;
- target-language or local-market evidence is missing.

Low confidence does not require inventing certainty. Recommend the next validation step or label the decision as a strategic hypothesis.

## 20. Stop conditions and escalation

Pause or limit the research when:

- the target query, market, language, or decision is undefined;
- access restrictions prevent inspection of essential pages;
- results are unsafe, irrelevant, or dominated by a different entity;
- the requested comparison would require private or proprietary data;
- a claim needs legal, medical, financial, security, or regulatory expertise beyond available authoritative evidence;
- Neetrino lacks the approved expertise, proof, or service fit needed for the proposed angle;
- the topic creates a substantial conflict with an existing page that has not been resolved;
- research findings materially change the intended scope and user approval is needed.

Do not keep collecting competitors after the decision is adequately supported. More browser tabs do not become insight through osmosis.

## 24. Decision rules after research

### Create

Create a page when the intent is relevant, the portfolio lacks a suitable owner, the gap is defensible, and Neetrino has adequate expertise or evidence.

### Update

Update when an existing page already owns the intent but is outdated, incomplete, poorly structured, weakly evidenced, or misaligned with current results.

### Merge

Merge when several Neetrino pages satisfy substantially the same task and no durable audience, intent, or page-role distinction exists.

### Split

Split when one page serves incompatible intents, audiences, or page types that the SERP and journey treat separately.

### Reposition

Reposition when the topic remains relevant but current results indicate a different dominant task, audience, or format.

### Reject or defer

Reject or defer when:

- intent is irrelevant to Neetrino's audience or services;
- the query is navigational for another brand;
- the proposed page cannot add material value;
- evidence or internal expertise is insufficient;
- local-language absence is the only opportunity signal;
- the topic would cannibalize a stronger page;
- the SERP is too volatile or ambiguous for the proposed investment;
- the correct asset is not currently feasible.

## 25. Anti-patterns

Never:

- treat the first ranking page as the required outline;
- call every ranking domain a direct competitor;
- inspect only titles and snippets;
- infer search volume, traffic, conversions, backlinks, or authority from rank alone;
- treat rank as proof of accuracy or quality;
- compare pages collected in different markets without noting the difference;
- average conflicting intent into a vague “comprehensive guide”;
- recommend more words because top pages are long;
- copy recurring headings merely because several competitors use them;
- confuse common misinformation with a required talking point;
- select a topic solely because competitors cover it;
- ignore Neetrino's existing page inventory;
- claim a gap before linking it to reader value;
- present third-party estimates as exact measurements;
- hide failed access, missing evidence, or low confidence;
- attack competitors instead of explaining fair trade-offs;
- promise ranking based on content parity or superiority.

## 26. Final SERP-and-competitor checklist

Before approving research or using it in a content brief, confirm:

### Research context

- [ ] Exact query, date, market, language, engine, and access conditions are recorded.
- [ ] The research supports a defined content or business decision.
- [ ] Material query variants were tested without artificial expansion.
- [ ] Observed findings are separated from inference and unknowns.

### Intent and SERP

- [ ] Dominant, secondary, mixed, fractured, local, or navigational intent is identified.
- [ ] Organic results and SERP features are classified separately.
- [ ] Dominant page types and audience levels are documented.
- [ ] AI answers, direct answers, local units, video, community, and other relevant surfaces are considered.
- [ ] The intended keyword cluster and page type were confirmed or revised.

### Competitor research

- [ ] Search competitors are distinguished from business competitors and primary sources.
- [ ] Representative pages were opened and inspected in context.
- [ ] Strengths, limitations, evidence quality, bias, and conversion role are recorded.
- [ ] No hidden analytics or proprietary facts are inferred without evidence.
- [ ] Competitor material has not been copied, translated, or structurally imitated.

### Opportunity and decision

- [ ] Every proposed gap maps to a real reader need.
- [ ] Neetrino can address the gap credibly and within approved service territory.
- [ ] Differentiation is specific and useful rather than cosmetic.
- [ ] Existing Neetrino pages and cannibalization were checked.
- [ ] The final action is create, update, merge, split, reposition, reject, or defer.
- [ ] Evidence needs, internal inputs, risks, and next validation steps are explicit.
- [ ] Confidence and freshness are labelled.

If any material item fails, do not present the research as production-ready.
