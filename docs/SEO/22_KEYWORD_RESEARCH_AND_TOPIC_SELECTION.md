# 22 — Keyword Research and Topic Selection

## 1. Role of this file

This file is the authoritative keyword-research and topic-selection reference for the Neetrino SEO Blog GPT. Use it whenever the task involves keyword discovery, keyword lists, topic ideas, editorial planning, content clusters, content-gap analysis, page recommendations, prioritization, localization, cannibalization review, or selection of the next content item to create.

It defines:

- what counts as a keyword, query pattern, entity, topic, and page opportunity;
- how to discover demand from several evidence sources;
- how to connect search behavior to audience, intent, services, and business goals;
- how to cluster variants by shared need rather than by wording alone;
- how to evaluate opportunity without inventing volume, difficulty, CPC, or trend data;
- how to prioritize topics using strategic and measurable criteria;
- how to choose the correct page type and avoid cannibalization;
- how to research Armenian, English, and Russian demand without literal-translation errors;
- how to treat zero-volume, emerging, niche, and AI-assisted discovery topics;
- what must be documented before a topic enters production.

This file does not contain live keyword metrics, rankings, competitor positions, SERP features, market demand, or trend data. Those facts change and require current research from identified sources.

## 2. Core operating principle

A keyword is evidence of language. A topic is a publishing decision.

Never select a topic because a phrase appears in a tool or has the largest reported volume. Translate research into a decision through this chain:

> business territory → audience problem → search behavior → intent → query cluster → SERP and content landscape → page role → evidence readiness → portfolio gap → priority

The winning topic is not necessarily the largest query. It is the most defensible opportunity to help a relevant reader make progress while strengthening a business-relevant area in which Neetrino can contribute credible judgment.

## 3. Required conceptual distinctions

### 3.1 Seed topic

A broad starting territory used for discovery, such as CRM automation, e-commerce integration, or custom software. It is not yet a recommended page.

### 3.2 Keyword

A phrase measured, observed, or proposed as a search target. A keyword can represent several intents, and several keywords can represent one intent.

### 3.3 Search query

The wording a real user enters or speaks. Query data may reveal language that keyword databases normalize or miss.

### 3.4 Query pattern

A reusable structure such as:

- how to choose [solution];
- [solution A] vs [solution B];
- why [process] fails;
- cost of [solution];
- [industry] automation examples.

Patterns support discovery but do not prove demand.

### 3.5 Entity

A distinct concept, product, standard, platform, role, industry, or system that search engines and readers must understand in context. Entity coverage supports clarity; it is not permission to add unrelated terminology.

### 3.6 Search intent

The progress the searcher wants to make. Apply `21_SEARCH_INTENT_AND_CUSTOMER_JOURNEY.md`; do not infer intent from modifiers alone.

### 3.7 Keyword cluster

A group of queries that can be satisfied by one page because they share the same primary need, answer shape, and likely destination.

### 3.8 Content topic

A defined editorial promise to a specific audience, with a primary intent, scope, page role, and desired next step.

### 3.9 Page opportunity

A justified recommendation to create, update, merge, redirect, reposition, or leave a page unchanged.

## 4. Research starts with strategic boundaries

Before collecting keywords, define:

- approved service or expertise territory;
- priority audience and buying role;
- business problem or decision;
- target market and language;
- website or content goal;
- known page inventory;
- available first-party evidence;
- exclusions and claim restrictions.

Use `10_NEETRINO_BRAND_PROFILE.md`, `12_NEETRINO_SERVICES_KNOWLEDGE.md`, `20_AUDIENCE_AND_BUYER_PERSONAS.md`, and `11_WEBSITE_CONTENT_AND_CONVERSION_GOALS.md`.

If these boundaries are missing, discovery may remain exploratory. Do not present the result as a finalized content roadmap.

## 5. Research modes

### 5.1 Exploratory research

Purpose: map an unfamiliar territory, vocabulary, problems, entities, and possible demand.

Output: hypotheses and candidate clusters, not final targets.

### 5.2 Validation research

Purpose: test whether a proposed topic has real audience, intent, SERP, portfolio, and evidence support.

Output: pursue, revise, defer, reject, or investigate further.

### 5.3 Prioritization research

Purpose: rank validated opportunities against business and editorial constraints.

Output: ordered backlog with reasoning and confidence.

### 5.4 Optimization research

Purpose: improve an existing page using query, performance, SERP, and content-gap evidence.

Output: update, expand, refocus, merge, split, or leave unchanged.

### 5.5 Portfolio-gap research

Purpose: identify missing journey stages, buyer questions, service territories, proof assets, or internal-link destinations.

Output: portfolio actions, not merely more blog titles.

Do not mix these modes silently. The required evidence and recommendation strength differ.

## 6. Discovery-source hierarchy

Use multiple source types when available.

### 6.1 First-party sources

- website search and analytics;
- Search Console query and page data;
- CRM, sales, and qualification questions;
- client calls, proposals, objections, and support requests;
- internal experts and delivery teams;
- existing content performance;
- on-site search, chatbot, or form language.

These sources reveal actual audience language and business relevance. They may be incomplete, biased toward current customers, or affected by existing visibility.

### 6.2 Search-platform sources

- autocomplete and related searches;
- People Also Ask or equivalent question surfaces;
- live SERPs and result types;
- search trend tools;
- platform-provided query data.

These sources reveal current search behavior and result expectations. Record market, language, device or location context, and research date where relevant.

### 6.3 Keyword databases

Use them for directional volume, competition, CPC, variants, and topic expansion. Name the provider and date. Treat modeled metrics as estimates, not census data.

### 6.4 Competitor and category sources

- competitor page inventories;
- ranking-page themes and formats;
- category publications and communities;
- software directories and review platforms;
- documentation, standards, and vendor resources.

Competitor coverage proves that content exists, not that Neetrino should copy it.

### 6.5 Direct audience and expert sources

Interviews, workshops, surveys, community discussions, and subject-matter experts can reveal latent demand and missing language. Distinguish observed evidence from individual opinion.

## 7. Source and metric integrity

Never invent or approximate:

- search volume;
- keyword difficulty;
- CPC;
- trend direction;
- ranking position;
- traffic potential;
- click-through rate;
- competitor traffic;
- conversion potential.

For every reported metric, retain:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Named platform, report, or dataset |
| Date | Research or export date |
| Market | Country or geography where applicable |
| Language | Query language or database setting |
| Device | Include when the source separates it |
| Unit | Monthly searches, index, percentage, currency, etc. |
| Match or scope | Exact, phrase, broad, grouped, or modeled |

When metrics are unavailable, use qualitative labels such as observed demand, first-party demand, emerging need, strategically relevant hypothesis, or unvalidated candidate.

Never turn `unknown` into `zero`.

## 8. Seed development

Build seeds from the intersection of:

- service capabilities;
- buyer problems and desired outcomes;
- journey stages and decision risks;
- industries and use cases;
- integrations, platforms, and technical dependencies;
- implementation, adoption, security, data, and maintenance concerns;
- comparison and alternative decisions;
- cost, timing, scope, team, and vendor questions.

Avoid generating seeds only from service names. Buyers often search for symptoms, workflows, failures, or decisions before they know the solution category.

Example:

> Weak seed set: CRM development, CRM company, custom CRM.
>
> Stronger territory: leads getting lost, sales follow-up process, CRM versus spreadsheets, CRM requirements, data migration, adoption failure, reporting, integrations, implementation cost, vendor evaluation.

## 9. Query expansion framework

Expand each seed through relevant lenses:

| Lens | Typical direction |
| --- | --- |
| Definition | what is, meaning, explained |
| Problem | why, causes, signs, mistakes |
| Diagnosis | audit, checklist, assessment |
| Outcome | improve, reduce, control, automate |
| Process | how to, steps, workflow |
| Options | types, approaches, alternatives |
| Comparison | versus, custom or ready-made |
| Evaluation | features, criteria, requirements |
| Commercial | cost, price, timeline, vendor |
| Risk | security, failure, migration, compliance |
| Implementation | setup, integration, adoption |
| Industry | healthcare, retail, education, etc. |
| Role | CEO, sales leader, operations, CTO |
| Platform or entity | API, ERP, payment, CRM platform |
| Post-launch | maintenance, analytics, optimization |

Use only lenses that represent plausible needs. Mechanical permutation is not research.

## 10. Audience-language interpretation

Searchers may describe:

- a symptom instead of a system;
- a tool instead of an outcome;
- an outcome without knowing the implementation;
- a technical term imprecisely;
- a local-market concept using English or Russian terminology;
- the same need differently according to role.

Map wording to the likely business task without rewriting the user's language into internal jargon too early.

For each important cluster, ask:

- Who would use this wording?
- What may have triggered the search?
- What do they already understand?
- What decision or task follows?
- What terminology should the page clarify without patronizing the reader?

## 11. Intent analysis before clustering

For each query or group, identify:

- explicit request;
- implicit business situation;
- likely reader or buying role;
- primary intent type;
- secondary or mixed intent;
- expected answer format;
- required evidence;
- appropriate page type;
- readiness for a commercial next step.

Apply the full model in `21_SEARCH_INTENT_AND_CUSTOMER_JOURNEY.md`.

Do not assume:

- “best” is always transactional;
- “cost” always means ready to buy;
- “how to” is always informational only;
- a branded platform query belongs on Neetrino's site;
- two similarly worded queries share intent.

## 12. SERP analysis

When live research is authorized and available, inspect the current results for each priority cluster.

Record:

- dominant page types;
- result formats and SERP features;
- recurring subtopics and entities;
- audience level and answer depth;
- freshness sensitivity;
- local versus international result mix;
- brand, vendor, publisher, forum, video, and documentation presence;
- conflicting interpretations of the query;
- weak, outdated, generic, or unanswered areas;
- whether the result set supports the assumed intent.

SERP similarity is evidence for clustering, not an automatic rule. Personalized, localized, unstable, or sparse SERPs require caution.

Do not call a SERP gap merely because competitors wrote shorter articles. A gap must represent unmet user value: clearer judgment, stronger evidence, a missing audience, a local context, a usable framework, a better comparison, or a genuinely unanswered question.

## 13. Keyword clustering rules

Group queries into one page when they share:

- the same primary task;
- compatible reader expectations;
- substantially overlapping result sets or answer requirements;
- one coherent editorial promise;
- the same suitable page type;
- no contradictory conversion path.

Separate them when they require:

- different primary intents;
- different audiences or expertise levels;
- different page types;
- materially different evidence;
- incompatible scopes;
- distinct service or location pages;
- separate journey steps that deserve independent ownership.

### 13.1 Primary target

The clearest phrase representing the cluster's dominant intent and page promise. It is not necessarily the highest-volume variant.

### 13.2 Close variants

Natural wording alternatives that the same page can answer without creating separate sections solely for repetition.

### 13.3 Supporting subtopics

Questions or concepts necessary to satisfy the main intent. They are not secondary primary keywords.

### 13.4 Excluded queries

Queries that are irrelevant, misleading, too broad, conflicting, unsafe, unsupported, or owned by another page.

## 14. Topic formulation

Convert a cluster into a topic using:

> audience + situation or decision + useful outcome + scope boundary + page role

A valid topic must state or imply:

- who it helps;
- what question or decision it resolves;
- why that question matters now;
- what the page will and will not cover;
- what makes the answer useful or distinctive;
- what journey step follows.

Do not use the keyword as the title by default. The title must express the page's honest promise and can be finalized later.

## 15. Page-type selection

Recommend the page type demanded by intent.

| Need | Likely destination |
| --- | --- |
| Understand a concept or problem | Educational article or guide |
| Diagnose readiness or failure | Checklist, assessment, or diagnostic guide |
| Compare approaches | Comparison or alternatives page |
| Evaluate a capability | Service, solution, or use-case page |
| Verify expertise | Case study, proof page, or technical explainer |
| Plan implementation | Implementation guide or resource |
| Choose a vendor | Vendor-selection or service page |
| Complete an action | Conversion, contact, tool, or product page |

Do not create a blog article to intercept a query better served by a service, case study, glossary, documentation, tool, landing, or existing page.

## 16. Existing-site and cannibalization review

Before recommending a new page, inspect the current inventory when available.

For each opportunity, choose one action:

- create;
- update;
- expand;
- refocus;
- consolidate;
- split;
- redirect;
- improve internal linking;
- retain unchanged;
- reject.

Potential cannibalization exists when multiple pages compete for the same primary intent and page role. Similar vocabulary alone is not cannibalization.

Assign one canonical owner for every primary cluster. Supporting pages must have distinct purposes and link relationships.

## 17. Content-gap analysis

Evaluate gaps across several dimensions:

### 17.1 Query gap

Relevant demand has no suitable page.

### 17.2 Intent gap

The topic exists, but the page answers a different task.

### 17.3 Journey gap

The portfolio covers discovery but lacks comparison, proof, implementation, or decision support.

### 17.4 Persona gap

Content exists for one stakeholder but ignores another decisive role.

### 17.5 Evidence gap

A claim or decision requires proof the current content does not provide.

### 17.6 Format gap

The need is better served by a checklist, calculator, template, case study, diagram, or service page than by another article.

### 17.7 Localization gap

Translated content does not reflect the local query language, context, terminology, or SERP.

Competitor absence alone does not prove opportunity, and competitor presence alone does not prove priority.

## 18. Topic-priority model

Evaluate every serious candidate against these factors:

| Factor | Core question |
| --- | --- |
| Audience fit | Does it matter to a priority segment or buying role? |
| Problem importance | Is the underlying task consequential and specific? |
| Search evidence | Is demand observed, modeled, first-party, emerging, or only assumed? |
| Intent clarity | Can one page satisfy the dominant need? |
| Brand fit | Can Neetrino contribute credible and relevant judgment? |
| Service proximity | Is the connection to an approved capability natural? |
| Journey value | Does it fill a meaningful decision-stage gap? |
| Business usefulness | Could it support qualified discovery, evaluation, trust, or demand? |
| Evidence readiness | Can the page be supported responsibly? |
| Distinctive value | Is there a defensible reason for this page to exist? |
| Page readiness | Is the correct destination type clear and feasible? |
| Internal-link value | Can it strengthen or be supported by the site architecture? |
| Portfolio fit | Does it improve balance rather than duplicate coverage? |
| Timing | Is there a justified seasonal, regulatory, product, or market reason? |
| Effort | Is the expected value proportionate to research and production cost? |
| Cannibalization risk | Can ownership be assigned cleanly? |

## 19. Scoring framework

When ranking a large backlog, score factors only to make judgment explicit. A score is not truth.

Recommended scale:

- `0` — absent, conflicting, or unknown;
- `1` — weak;
- `2` — moderate;
- `3` — strong;
- `4` — exceptional and well-supported.

Use separate groups:

### Strategic value

- audience fit;
- problem importance;
- brand and service fit;
- journey and business value.

### Search opportunity

- demand evidence;
- intent clarity;
- SERP opportunity;
- trend or timing evidence.

### Execution confidence

- evidence readiness;
- distinctiveness;
- page and internal-link readiness;
- production feasibility.

### Risk deductions

- cannibalization;
- claim risk;
- weak service relevance;
- unstable or misleading demand;
- excessive maintenance burden.

Weights must reflect the current objective. Record them whenever a numeric score is shown. Do not mix sourced metrics and judgment scores as if they have equal precision.

Any topic with a critical failure—brand conflict, wrong audience, unsupported claims, unsafe advice, or unresolvable intent conflict—must be rejected regardless of total score.

## 20. Priority tiers

Use these default decision labels:

### Priority A — Produce or optimize next

Strong strategic fit, clear need, credible value, suitable page, and sufficient evidence.

### Priority B — Valuable backlog

Good opportunity, but a dependency, stronger proof, a destination page, or a higher-priority gap should come first.

### Priority C — Explore or monitor

Promising but weakly validated, emerging, niche, or dependent on more research.

### Reject or exclude

Irrelevant, misleading, duplicative, unsupported, wrong-intent, or commercially forced.

Always provide the reason. A tier without rationale is decorative project management.

## 21. Zero-volume and low-volume topics

Do not reject a topic solely because a database reports zero or low volume.

It may still deserve priority when it:

- answers repeated first-party questions;
- supports a high-value buying decision;
- addresses a niche but qualified audience;
- represents emerging terminology or technology;
- fills a critical journey or proof gap;
- supports a strategically important service page;
- has meaningful long-tail or aggregated demand;
- can become a cited reference in AI-assisted discovery;
- is essential for customer success or implementation.

Label the demand basis accurately. Do not call strategic relevance organic-search demand.

## 22. High-volume topic caution

High volume can still be a poor opportunity when:

- intent is too broad or consumer-oriented;
- geography or language is wrong;
- results are dominated by definitions, tools, or platforms Neetrino cannot credibly displace;
- the topic has weak service or buyer relevance;
- the query would attract unqualified traffic;
- a useful answer requires evidence Neetrino does not have;
- the maintenance burden is disproportionate;
- the query's dominant meaning differs from the proposed topic.

Traffic potential is not business value, and business value is not guaranteed conversion.

## 23. Emerging and time-sensitive topics

For new technologies, regulations, platform changes, and market events:

- verify the current state and effective date;
- distinguish announced, beta, active, deprecated, and speculative status;
- prefer primary sources;
- state uncertainty;
- record a review date;
- assess update burden before publishing;
- avoid trend-chasing without audience and service fit.

Freshness can increase priority, but it also increases maintenance responsibility.

## 24. Multilingual keyword research

Research Armenian, English, and Russian as distinct search environments.

For each language:

- observe native phrasing and mixed-language terminology;
- inspect local SERPs where possible;
- identify transliteration and spelling variants;
- distinguish borrowed technical terms from natural explanation;
- account for different demand, competition, and intent;
- map equivalent concepts rather than literal keyword strings;
- decide whether the language needs an equivalent, adapted, or unique page.

Do not transfer volume or difficulty from one language to another. Do not assume that the English primary keyword should be inserted into Armenian or Russian copy unchanged.

For Armenian research, consider Armenian-script, English technical, Russian-influenced, and transliterated wording only when observed or plausibly used. Avoid stuffing every variant into the page.

## 25. Local-market and international scope

Separate:

- Armenia-specific demand;
- Armenian-language demand from broader geography;
- English or Russian demand within Armenia;
- international demand relevant to Neetrino;
- generic global interest with no practical business fit.

Location modifiers do not automatically require separate pages. Create local pages only when intent, offering, evidence, or user value materially differs.

## 26. AI-assisted and zero-click discovery

Topic selection must account for discovery through AI answers, snippets, knowledge panels, and other low-click environments.

Prefer topics where Neetrino can provide:

- clear definitions and distinctions;
- decision frameworks;
- concise factual answers supported by deeper analysis;
- original examples, experience, or evidence;
- structured comparisons;
- explicit assumptions and limitations;
- quotable but non-promotional expert judgment.

Do not create shallow “answer snippets” that make the full page unnecessary. The page must add depth, application, evidence, or decision value beyond the extractable answer.

## 27. Topic portfolio balance

Review the backlog across:

- services and business priorities;
- audiences and buying roles;
- problem, solution, comparison, validation, implementation, and vendor-selection intent;
- discovery, evaluation, proof, commitment, and adoption stages;
- broad pillars and specific questions;
- evergreen and time-sensitive content;
- blog, service, case study, resource, and conversion pages;
- Armenian, English, and Russian coverage;
- authority building and qualified commercial usefulness.

Do not impose a universal percentage split. Correct imbalance according to current goals and evidence.

## 28. Topic dependencies and sequencing

Publish in an order that creates a usable information architecture.

Typical dependencies include:

- service page before supporting commercial articles;
- pillar or canonical guide before narrow supporting pages;
- foundational definition before advanced comparison;
- proof asset before strong outcome claims;
- implementation guide after solution evaluation coverage;
- destination page before a CTA or internal-link path points to it.

A lower-scoring foundation may precede a higher-scoring article when the latter depends on it.

## 29. Required research workflow

Follow this sequence:

1. Define the business territory, audience, market, language, and research mode.
2. Review relevant services, personas, intent stages, conversion goals, and existing pages.
3. Generate seed territories from problems, outcomes, decisions, entities, and use cases.
4. Collect observed queries and candidate language from appropriate sources.
5. Preserve source, date, market, language, and metric context.
6. Infer explicit, implicit, and latent intent.
7. Inspect live SERPs for priority candidates when current validation is required.
8. Cluster queries by shared task, expected answer, and page role.
9. Map each cluster to an existing or proposed canonical page.
10. Identify portfolio, intent, evidence, format, and localization gaps.
11. Evaluate strategic value, search opportunity, execution confidence, and risks.
12. Assign priority and confidence.
13. Define dependencies, internal-link role, and next step.
14. Produce the required keyword and topic brief.
15. Recheck cannibalization, evidence status, and claim boundaries before production.

Do not skip directly from tool export to article titles.

## 32. Confidence labels

### High confidence

Multiple current sources agree; intent and page role are clear; strategic and evidence fit are strong; site ownership is known.

### Medium confidence

The opportunity is plausible and supported, but one important variable—metrics, SERP stability, page inventory, localization, or evidence—is incomplete.

### Low confidence

The topic is exploratory, inferred from limited evidence, or dependent on unverified assumptions.

Confidence describes the recommendation, not writing enthusiasm.

## 33. Rejection and deferral rules

Reject a topic when it:

- conflicts with Neetrino's approved positioning or capabilities;
- targets a non-priority or irrelevant audience without strategic reason;
- has a dominant intent Neetrino cannot satisfy;
- duplicates an existing canonical page without a distinct role;
- depends on fabricated, unavailable, unsafe, or unprovable claims;
- is selected only because a competitor ranks for it;
- would mislead readers about services, outcomes, pricing, or expertise;
- creates disproportionate update or legal risk.

Defer rather than reject when the opportunity is valid but awaits:

- live research;
- expert input;
- case evidence;
- a service or destination page;
- analytics access;
- local-language validation;
- a product, platform, or regulatory milestone.

## 34. Anti-patterns

Do not:

- sort a keyword export by volume and call it a strategy;
- create one page per spelling or close variant;
- combine unrelated intents because phrases share a noun;
- treat keyword difficulty as an objective probability of ranking;
- assume CPC proves B2B value or conversion;
- copy competitor titles, structures, or claims;
- recommend only top-of-funnel informational articles;
- force every topic toward a sales CTA;
- call every missing competitor topic a content gap;
- report modeled numbers without source and date;
- infer zero demand from missing database data;
- translate keywords literally across languages;
- ignore the current site inventory;
- create a blog post where a service page, case study, tool, or update is required;
- optimize for keyword repetition instead of question satisfaction;
- publish a time-sensitive topic without an update plan;
- confuse search visibility with qualified business impact.

## 35. Final keyword-and-topic checklist

Before approving a recommendation, confirm:

### Strategic fit

- [ ] The service or expertise territory is approved.
- [ ] A priority audience, problem, or decision is clear.
- [ ] The topic supports a defined website or portfolio goal.
- [ ] Neetrino's connection is natural and credible.

### Research integrity

- [ ] Metrics have named sources, dates, markets, languages, and units.
- [ ] Missing data is labeled unknown rather than zero.
- [ ] Observed evidence and strategic hypothesis are separated.
- [ ] Time-sensitive claims were checked currently.

### Intent and clustering

- [ ] Primary and mixed intents are identified.
- [ ] The cluster can be satisfied by one coherent page.
- [ ] Variants, subtopics, and exclusions are separated.
- [ ] The correct page type is selected.

### Portfolio and execution

- [ ] Existing pages and cannibalization were reviewed.
- [ ] The canonical page owner is clear.
- [ ] Evidence and expertise are available or explicitly required.
- [ ] A distinctive user-value opportunity exists.
- [ ] Internal-link and journey roles are defined.
- [ ] Dependencies and maintenance needs are known.

### Prioritization

- [ ] Priority reflects strategic value, search evidence, execution confidence, and risk.
- [ ] Any score has declared factors and weights.
- [ ] Confidence is stated with a reason.
- [ ] Rejected and deferred candidates have explicit reasons.

## 36. Final operating rule

Choose topics where relevant demand, business value, credible expertise, clear intent, the correct page role, and sufficient evidence overlap.

If one of those elements is missing, do not disguise the gap with a larger keyword list. Research it, defer it, reshape it, or reject it.
